# 04 Billing Basics

Education only. This chapter explains concepts in plain language — it is not
medical billing or coding advice. Always confirm billing steps with your
clinic's billing manager or a certified medical coder before changing any
workflow.

## What AI can help with (administrative side only)

- Drafting patient-friendly explanations of bills and statements
- Writing plain-language superbill templates (for your coder to complete)
- Drafting payment-plan letters and collection-friendly reminders
- Summarizing payer explanation of benefits (EOB) documents — with fake data
  only, never real patient EOBs
- Creating a billing glossary for front-desk staff

## What AI should NOT do

- Do not let AI assign CPT, HCPCS, or ICD-10 codes. Coding requires training
  and certification.
- Do not let AI decide whether a claim should be paid or denied.
- Do not paste real claims, remittance advices, or patient billing details
  into an AI tool.
- Do not submit claims based on AI output.

## Key billing concepts (education only)

| Term | Plain-English meaning |
|------|----------------------|
| Superbill | A form a provider fills out listing services and diagnoses; used by the patient or biller to create a claim |
| CPT code | A number describing a medical service or procedure (maintained by the AMA) |
| ICD-10-CM code | A number describing a diagnosis or reason for the visit |
| HCPCS code | A code for supplies, equipment, and some services (Medicare uses these heavily) |
| Claim | The bill sent to an insurance payer for services |
| EOB | Explanation of Benefits — the payer's statement showing what was paid, denied, or applied to deductible |
| Remittance advice | The payer's payment detail that accompanies claim payment |
| Deductible / copay / coinsurance | Amounts the patient owes before or alongside insurance payment |

**Important:** Code sets change every year. Payers have their own rules. Only
a certified coder or biller should assign codes or submit claims.

## Step-by-step: draft a superbill template (fake data only)

A superbill is not a claim. It is a document the provider completes so a
biller (or patient) can build a claim.

1. Open a new AI chat. Use fake provider and patient details:
   - Provider: Dr. Rivera, Example Family Clinic, 123 Example Street,
     Suite 4, Exampletown, IN 46000, NPI: 1234567893 (example)
   - Patient: Jordan Example, DOB 05/12/1988, visit date 04/10/2026
2. Prompt:

   > "Draft a blank superbill template for a primary care office visit.
   > Include fields for: provider name, clinic address, NPI (example number),
   > patient name, DOB, visit date, place of service, CPT code (blank field),
   > ICD-10-CM diagnosis code (blank field), charges, provider signature, and
   > a disclaimer: 'This is an education-only template, not a completed claim.
   > Codes must be assigned by certified staff.' Keep it clean and printable."

3. Review the template. Add or remove fields your clinic needs.
4. Have your billing manager or certified coder approve it before use.
5. Print blank copies. Never fill in real codes based on AI suggestions.

## Payment posting and patient statements (administrative)

AI can help draft the *letter* that goes with a statement, not the math:

- Draft friendly past-due reminder letters
- Draft payment-plan offer letters
- Write plain-language "what this charge means" inserts for statements

A staff member must verify amounts, dates, and balances before any letter
goes out.

## Compliance reminder for billing

Billing mistakes can look like fraud even when they are accidental. Never
upcode (bill for a more expensive service than provided), never bill for
services not performed, and always follow your clinic's compliance plan. When
in doubt, ask your compliance officer.

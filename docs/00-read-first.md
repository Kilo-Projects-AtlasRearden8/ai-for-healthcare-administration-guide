# Read First: Ground Rules

Welcome to the AI for Healthcare Administration beginner manual. This chapter
covers the most important rules before you touch any AI tool at work.

## 1. Education only — never medical or legal advice

Everything in this manual is for education only. It is not medical advice,
not legal advice, not compliance advice, and not medical billing or coding
advice. It does not replace your clinic's policies, your compliance officer,
your attorney, or a certified medical coder.

Before you change any form, billing workflow, or documentation practice at
your clinic, check with the right person at your organization.

## 2. Never paste patient data into an AI tool

Do not type or upload patient names, dates of birth, medical record numbers,
insurance IDs, phone numbers, addresses, diagnoses, or any other identifying
information into a public AI chatbot or tool.

Assume that anything you type into an AI tool could be seen by the tool's
company, stored, or used to train future models. Treat patient data like it
is public.

Use fake, made-up examples when you practice with AI. Every example in this
manual uses clearly fake patient data (names like "Jane Example," dates like
01/01/2000).

## 3. Follow your clinic's policies first

Your clinic may already have rules about AI, HIPAA, software approval, and
patient privacy. Clinic policy always wins over anything in this manual. If
your clinic says no AI tools for certain tasks, respect that.

Ask your compliance officer:
- Are we allowed to use AI tools at work?
- Which tools are approved?
- Do we have a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) with the vendor?
- Who do I ask before trying something new?

## 4. AI makes mistakes — always review

AI can sound confident and still be wrong. It can miss details, invent facts,
and get billing codes wrong. A human must review every AI output before it
goes near a patient record, a claim, or a form.

You are the human in the loop. You are responsible for the final result.

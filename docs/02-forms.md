# 02 Forms: Intake, Consent, and Office Forms

AI can help you draft, rewrite, and review office forms — but a human always
reviews the final version, and your clinic's legal counsel should approve any
form patients sign.

## What AI can help with

- Rewriting long, confusing forms in plain English
- Drafting new patient intake forms, consent forms, HIPAA acknowledgment
  sheets, and financial responsibility forms
- Creating checklists for front-desk staff
- Translating form instructions into simpler reading levels
- Drafting emails that go with forms ("Please complete the attached form
  before your first visit")

## What AI cannot do (and should not do)

- AI cannot give legal advice. Have a qualified attorney review any form that
  patients sign, especially consent, financial responsibility, and privacy
  notices.
- AI does not know your state's laws or your payer contracts.
- Never put a real patient's information into a form draft in an AI tool.

## Step-by-step: draft a new-patient intake form with AI

Use fake data only. Example patient: Jordan Example, born 05/12/1988.

1. **Open a new chat** with your AI tool. Do not reuse an old conversation.
2. **Paste a prompt like this:**

   > "I work at a small clinic. Draft a one-page new-patient intake form in
   > plain English. Include: patient name (fake example: Jordan Example), date
   > of birth (example: 05/12/1988), phone, email, emergency contact, reason
   > for visit, current medications (list format), allergies, and a short
   > HIPAA acknowledgment line. Add a note at the top: 'This is a draft —
   > have it reviewed by our compliance officer and legal counsel before use.'
   > Keep the tone warm and respectful."

3. **Read the output carefully.** Check:
   - Is the language simple enough? (Aim for about an 8th-grade reading level.)
   - Are all fields your clinic needs included?
   - Is the HIPAA language accurate? (Your compliance officer must confirm.)
4. **Rewrite anything awkward** in your own words.
5. **Send to your compliance officer and legal counsel** for approval before
   printing or using with patients.
6. **Save the approved version** as a template. Do not edit it casually.

## Printable form habits

- Keep a master copy of each approved form; staff work from copies.
- Label every form with version date and "Approved by compliance on ___."
- Store blank forms where patients cannot help themselves to filled-in ones.
- Shred drafts and misprints containing any real patient information.
